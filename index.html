The HARVEST project introduces an innovative framework to evaluate early prediction systems and response management strategies for maize and rice across the United States, Australia, India, and Japan. It addresses critical agrarian challenges, including insect pest and disease detection, drought response, climate adaptation, precision fertilization, and irrigation optimization across diverse global ecosystems.

By deploying IoT sensors, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), and digital twins, HARVEST utilizes standardized, cross-regional protocols to significantly enhance agricultural efficiency, resilience, and sustainability. Concurrently, the framework strictly governs large-scale farm data collection to guarantee transparency, equitable stakeholder participation, and sustained trust.

Research

Four integrated thrusts, each decomposed into cross-national tasks with designated Lead and Supporting teams

01
Detection and Mitigation of Biotic and Abiotic Stress
1.1  Automated FAW Trap & Drone Bio-pesticide Spraying

Automated trapping systems for Fall Armyworm (FAW) detection combined with drone-based bio-pesticide delivery for precision pest control in the field.

Lead: USA
Support: India
1.2  In-field Molecular Diagnostics & Spectral Disease Detection

LAMP-based molecular diagnostics coupled with spectral analysis for rapid, in-field identification of crop diseases and pathogens.

Lead: India
Support: USA
1.3  Spectral Analytics for Nutrient Stress in Rice

Hyperspectral and multispectral imaging pipelines to identify and quantify nutrient deficiencies and abiotic stress responses in rice crops.

Lead: India
Support: USA
02
Digital Twin for Data Collection, Computation and Prediction
2.1  AI-Driven Agricultural Digital Twin Design

Design and modeling of high-fidelity digital twins of agricultural environments for simulation, monitoring, and predictive analytics.

Lead: Japan
Support: USA, India
2.2  UAV-based Edge Computing & Rural Field Connectivity

Low-latency edge computing pipelines aboard UAVs with resilient rural connectivity frameworks for real-time data collection in remote fields.

Lead: India
Support: Japan, USA
2.3  Ephemeral-Orchestrated Secure Distributed Learning

Privacy-preserving, ephemeral federated learning architectures for secure, distributed model training across heterogeneous agricultural nodes.

Lead: USA
Support: USA, India
03
Multimodal LLMs-based Transfer Learning
3.1  Agriculture Large Vision-Language Models

Development and fine-tuning of multimodal large vision-language models (VLMs) tailored to agricultural image understanding, diagnosis, and advisory.

Lead: USA
Support: Australia, India
3.2  Few- and Zero-Shot Agriculture Adaptation

Cross-domain transfer and few/zero-shot adaptation strategies enabling models to generalize across crops, geographies, and environmental conditions with minimal labeled data.

Lead: USA
Support: Australia, India
04
Evaluation and Validation
4.1  Multimodal Data Infrastructure

Standardized, cross-regional data collection infrastructure spanning satellite imagery, IoT sensors, UAV feeds, and laboratory data for consistent benchmarking.

Lead: All Collaborative Nations
Support: All Collaborative Nations
4.2  Real-time Diagnostics & Digital Twin Integration

Tight coupling of real-time field diagnostics with digital twin simulations for continuous validation and adaptation of predictive models.

Lead: Japan
Support: All Collaborative Nations
4.4  Field Pilots & Scale-up Across Regions

End-to-end validation pilots across diverse agricultural environments in the US, India, Australia, and Japan to test scalability and regional adaptability of the HARVEST framework.

Lead: All Collaborative Nations
Support: All Collaborative Nations
